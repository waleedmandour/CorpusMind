1 · Getting started
From raw texts to analysis in three steps

CorpusMind is a local-first research environment: your data never leaves your machine, and the grounded assistant answers only with evidence from your own corpora. Open Your Corpus and work top to bottom:

1Create a project & corpus

Optional learner facets: the learners' L1 and a CEFR level (A1–C2) at creation — they power the new group-by views.

2Upload texts

Drag in TXT, DOCX, PDF, HTML or XML — the engine builds a versioned annotation layer (tokens, lemmas, POS, morphology).

3Pick a tool

Concordance, Frequency, Collocation, Keyness, N-grams, Dispersion and more — every result carries its full parameters.

Tip: press Ctrl/Cmd + K for the command palette. The floating AI assistant (bottom-right) is context-aware on every tool screen — in v1.2.0 its panel is wider and wraps model output properly.

3 · New analysis tool
Vector KWIC — concordancing with embeddingsNEW

Following Anthony (2025), Mode A takes a node word plus a research query: every KWIC line containing the node is re-ranked by cosine similarity between its context window and your query, so the lines that mean what you are looking for surface first. Mode B leaves the node empty and just asks a question — the corpus is scanned sentence-by-sentence (capped at 6,000, always reported) for the top-k most similar sentences.

One-click setup — and an honesty rule

Lines are embedded with a local model — recommended: ollama pull bge-m3 (multilingual, strong Arabic), also in Settings → Models → Embedding; a missing model triggers a one-click pull hint. The Similarity column is raw cosine similarity — closer means more relevant to your query, never a confidence score. Vectors are cached per corpus and model; exports include similarity.

4 · New research suite
Learner Research — CAF, CIA and error candidatesNEW

The Learner Research group (أبحاث لغة المتعلم) brings learner-corpus methodology into the app — exportable, with formulas and citations shown in the interface.

CAF Report

Complexity–Accuracy–Fluency per corpus or grouped by L1 / CEFR: TTR, MATTR, MTLD, HD-D, Guiraud; EN clause indices from the dependency parser; Arabic word-length and root-richness proxies; a labelled error-free-sentence heuristic.

CIA Compare

Contrastive Interlanguage Analysis (Granger 1998): learner corpus vs reference (engine corpus or bundled list), or group vs group — keyness plus CAF deltas in one exportable report.

Error Patterns

Rule-based error candidates, never confirmed errors: EN articles, prepositions, agreement, spelling; AR hamza variants, ة/ه and ى/ي confusions. Each links to its exact line — you verify before counting.

AI-vs-learner comparator

Paste an AI text on the same prompt: the identical CAF battery runs on it — a side-by-side delta table against your learner corpus (cf. the 2025 LLM-vs-student-writing studies).

5 · Arabic-first search
Arabic normalization across the toolsNEW

The Concordancer, Frequency, Collocation and Keyness tools now carry an “Arabic normalization” toggle: hamza spellings unify (أ إ آ → ا), teh marbuta (ة → ه), alef maksura (ى → ي), and diacritics are stripped before matching or counting — spelling variants collapse into one row without re-ingesting anything. Regex always runs on raw text (and the tool says so); the same flag exists inside Vector KWIC.

6 · Models & AI
Settings: curated lists, Hugging Face, fit badgesNEW

Settings → Models separates text and embedding models with filter chips (bge-m3 is the recommended embedder); vision models moved to Lens. The new Hugging Face explorer searches live GGUF models sorted by real downloads, shows actual per-quantisation file sizes, and labels each with a machine-aware fit badge — GPU · CPU · Tight · Too-big — from real RAM/VRAM (rule-of-thumb, never a guarantee). Pulling (hf.co/<user>/<repo>:<quant>) reuses the familiar pull-progress flow.

7 · The CorpusMind family
Companion apps: Lens & VoiceNEW

CorpusMind is deliberately modular: the main app masters text; the companions extend it to images and audio — all three read the same engine and data directory, so projects, corpora and settings are shared.

CorpusMind Lens — vision

Vision-LM multimodal discourse analysis: image sets with OCR, Visual Grammar (Kress & van Leeuwen), image–text alignment, and a cross-modal AI that reads texts and images together. The parent-app Vision tab was removed in v1.2.0 — the workbench lives here now. waleedmandour.org/projects/CorpusMindLens

CorpusMind Voice — audio

Audio-to-corpus: transcribe recordings, build spoken-corpus data, and analyse it with the same battery of tools — closing the loop between written and spoken data in one research environment. waleedmandour.org/projects/CorpusMindVoice
